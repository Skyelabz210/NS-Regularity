## [EXPLORE T2] RESEARCH QUESTION

**Original Question:**
> Does lane-level commutativity failure correlate with specific algebraic properties of the generator (bit-shift ↔ GF(2) linearity, quadratic ↔ QR structure, compound ↔ cross-correlation)?

**Answer: YES, weakly.**

**Evidence:**

1. **GF(2)-Linear Correlation (LFSR vs XorShift)**
   - LFSR-16 (Galois, GF(2)-linear): 57.7% avg consistency
   - XorShift32 (arbitrary bit-shift): 18.5% avg consistency
   - **Ratio: 3.1× higher consistency for GF(2)**
   - Interpretation: Linearity over GF(2) partially preserved in modular reduction; bit-shift obscures it

2. **Composition Variance (Compound LCG)**
   - Simple LCG: 100% uniform across lanes (all 5 at 100%)
   - Compound LCG: 0% overall but high variance (9–66% per lane)
   - **Variance: 57.3 percentage points**
   - Interpretation: Different LCG pairs modulating different lanes → per-lane failures at different points

3. **Output Masking Non-Distinction (Reel vs Truncated)**
   - Reel-Mapped: 0% (all lanes fail)
   - Truncated: 0% (all lanes fail)
   - **No sub-class separation possible at T2**
   - Interpretation: Output masking destroys lane-level structure uniformly

**Conclusion:** Lane-level commutativity serves as a coarse-grained classifier (LCG vs non-LCG) but offers limited algebraic sub-class resolution without higher-order tools (T3 Hydra oracles, T6 Chimera schemas).
