# A curl-preserving diffusion step and a nonlinear weak limit

The original split vorticity step can leave the integer centered-curl image after one positive-viscosity update. This note gives two repairs at different levels. The first retains a common denominator during linear heat evolution. The second evolves a divergence-free rational velocity, with conservative advection and backward Euler viscosity. For the second scheme a written subsequence argument reaches a periodic Leray–Hopf weak solution for general divergence-free finite-energy initial data. An explicit small-data threshold yields a global smooth limit, and the same argument gives eventual regularity for general finite-energy data. Global smoothness from the initial time for arbitrary smooth three-dimensional data is a further question.

| Result | Verification and scope |
|---|---|
| Heat commutes with centered curl and divergence through every iterate | Lean, for translations on any additive commutative group. The original N8 curl, divergence and single floor observation are identified in Lean. |
| The face transport does zero energy work and backward Euler dissipates energy | Lean, for a balanced finite oriented face graph and a step satisfying its tested balance. The finite-dimensional rational invertibility lemma is also checked. The identification of the full grid scheme with these lemmas is written below. |
| Nonlinear solver and exact velocity–vorticity relation | Standard-library Python reference solving the coupled rational equations and checking the residual, divergence, means, energy, curl witness, and a nonlinear effect at odd and even periods. |
| Small-data regularity | Written proof with threshold $108\|\nabla u_0\|_2<\nu$; Lean checks absorption after the work estimate. |
| Strong space-time compactness, weak momentum equation and Leray–Hopf energy inequality | Written proof for the velocity scheme in §2. The original rounded Lean and N32 Rust evolutions have different updates. The continuum argument has not been formalized in Lean. |

## 1. Carry the denominator through the heat step

At a given mesh, represent a field by integer numerators $f$ and a positive common scale $A$. Its physical values are $f/A$. With integer weights $c,d$, $d>0$, the step sends $(f,A)$ to $(Hf,dA)$, where

$$Hf(x)=(d-6c)f(x)+c\sum_{y\sim x} f(y).$$

All translations commute with $H$. In particular $\mathrm{curl}_0 H u=H\mathrm{curl}_0u$ and $\mathrm{div}_0 Hu=H\mathrm{div}_0u$, using the raw centered differences. If the old integer vorticity has a divergence-free integer velocity witness, $H^ku$ is such a witness for $H^kw$ for every $k$. The physical scale is $d^kA$. Under $0\le6c\le d$, $d^{-k}H^k$ preserves the original enclosing interval. Common factors can be cancelled jointly from the scale and all carried numerators, including the witness. For one step, flooring $Hf/d$ gives the original update. Repeated flooring changes the evolution, as the [mass defect](NS_REFINEMENT.md) demonstrates. The Theory-of-the-One interpretation is explicit here. For a single numerator the [signed adjacent observation](bridges/theory_observations/NSObservations.lean) recovers its quotient within the specified window. Flooring discards the remainder before the next evolution. Retaining the common scale keeps the rational state exact, and requires no stored winding coordinate.

On the existing N32 shear fixture with $D=(32\cdot10000)^2$, the shared-scale heat numerator has total zero on each parity class. Measured against that exact rational step in input grid units, the canonical and CRT Rust outputs both have maximum error $2/D$. The floor update has maximum error $(D-1)/D$. This is a one-step comparison on the signed shear at the production parameters. [Rust test](https://github.com/Skyelabz210/NS-3D-solver_n32-ntt6/blob/308841c9499e43c03d7c90400407ca215c1fd0af/arena/crates/ns_v5/tests/rounded_shear.rs)

Mass conservation alone cannot certify the integer curl image. For the proposed face-rounded heat step at $N=8,c/d=1/6$, take $u_z(y)=(0,0,3,3,0,0,0,0)$ and $u_x=u_y=0$. Its raw x-curl is $(0,3,3,-3,-3,0,0,0)$. Taking a single oriented face flux $\operatorname{trunc}_0((w_{j+1}-w_j)/6)$ gives $(0,3,2,-2,-3,0,0,0)$. The even-row sum is $-1$ and the odd-row sum $+1$. Every component of a periodic centered curl sums to zero on each of the eight parity classes, because its two shifted sums over a class run through identical neighbours. On each fixed x/z parity class the example repeats the line result sixteen times. Thus every centered-curl representation is excluded after this face-rounded step.

## 2. A fully specified nonlinear velocity update

Work on the unit periodic three-torus, $N\ge3$, $h=1/N$. Write $D^+_jv=(v_{i+e_j}-v_i)/h$, $D^0_jv=(v_{i+e_j}-v_{i-e_j})/(2h)$, and $\Delta_h$ for the nearest-neighbour Laplacian. Use the cell inner product $\langle u,v\rangle_h=h^3\sum_i u_i\cdot v_i$. Let $Z_h=\{v:\sum_jD_j^0v_j=0\}$, with all constant velocities allowed. For an oriented face $i\to r=i+e_j$ write $A_{ir}=(a_{i,j}+a_{r,j})/2$ and $B_{ir}=(b_i+b_r)/2$. With old velocity $a$, define $b$ as the unique element of $Z_h$ satisfying, for every $v\in Z_h$,

$$\langle b-a,v\rangle_h+\tau c_h(a;b,v)+\tau\nu_h\langle D^+ b,D^+v\rangle_h=0,\qquad
 c_h(a;b,v)=h^3\sum_{i,j} A_{ir} B_{ir}\cdot(v_i-v_r)/h.$$

The advector is $a$ and the transported velocity is $b$. The weak flux sign above gives $+\mathrm{div}(a\otimes b)$ in the cell update, with flux component $a_j b_\ell$. The stencil uses the forward gradient for viscous dissipation. This controls oscillations near the centered derivative's blind frequencies. Mean velocity is conserved by testing with constants. For any $a\in Z_h$, the net outgoing face flux divided by $h$ is $\sum_jD^0_j a_j=0$ at each cell. Hence

$$c_h(a;b,b)=\frac{h^2}{2}\sum_{i,j} A_{ir}(|b_i|^2-|b_r|^2)=0.$$

The bilinear step operator at fixed $a$ therefore has diagonal $\|z\|_h^2+\tau\nu_h\|D^+z\|_h^2$ on $Z_h$. Its kernel is zero. Finite dimensionality gives a unique solution for every input and every $\nu_h,\tau>0$. For rational data, $h,\nu_h,\tau$, the divergence constraint matrix, a basis of its nullspace, and the reduced bilinear system are rational. Nondegeneracy is the same over the rationals and reals, so the output is exactly rational. With any common denominator $A$ for $b$, $U=Ab$ is an integer divergence-free witness and its raw integer curl $W$ represents physical vorticity $N W/(2A)$. This statement applies after every nonlinear step. The weak formulation eliminates pressure. The executable instead uses a saddle system and removes the redundant pressure equations. At odd $N$ the pressure kernel is the constants. At even $N$ it comprises constants on each parity class, since $D_j^0p=0$ means invariance under translation by $2e_j$.

Testing with $b$ and summing gives the exact identity

$$\|u^m\|_h^2+\sum_{n<m}\|u^{n+1}-u^n\|_h^2+2\nu_h\sum_{n<m}\tau\|D^+u^{n+1}\|_h^2=\|u^0\|_h^2.$$

This is a global energy-class estimate for the full nonlinear discretization, uniform in both resolutions. The finite stored integers can grow substantially. A single test step used common denominators of 453 bits at $N=3$ and 679 bits at $N=4$. These sizes measure the specified fixtures. No asymptotic denominator estimate is assumed. Any fixed-width CRT realization needs an independently justified range or an exact extension mechanism.

## 3. Passage to the continuum

**Theorem (written proof).** For any $u_0\in L^2(\mathbb T^3)^3$ with distributional divergence zero, choose rational $u_h^0\in Z_h$ whose piecewise-constant interpolants converge strongly in $L^2$ to $u_0$. Take $N\to\infty$, $\tau\to0$ and positive rational $\nu_h\to\nu>0$ without a mesh–time coupling. The right-endpoint piecewise-constant velocities have a subsequence converging strongly in $L^2((0,T)\times\mathbb T^3)$ for every finite $T$ to a Leray–Hopf solution of unforced periodic Navier–Stokes with initial velocity $u_0$. The left-endpoint velocities converge to the same limit. All norms and bounds in this section are in physical units.

**Initial data.** Such rational initial states exist. Apply the orthogonal projector $P_h$ onto $Z_h$ to rational approximations of the cell averages with $o(1)$ discrete $L^2$ error. This projector has rational entries, by the rational basis/Gram matrix formula. Its discrete Fourier multiplier is projection onto $s_h(k)^\perp$, where $s_{h,j}(k)=h^{-1}\sin(2\pi k_jh)$, and is the identity when $s_h=0$. For a fixed solenoidal trigonometric polynomial these projected cell averages converge in $L^2$ to the polynomial, since $s_h(k)\to2\pi k$. Density and contraction of both cell averaging and projection give the assertion for $L^2$ data. The zero Fourier mode is retained.

**Spatial tails.** Take discrete Fourier coefficients with normalized grid measure and centered frequency representatives $-N/2\le k_j<N/2$ (round endpoints to the contained integers at odd $N$). Parseval gives forward-gradient weight $\lambda_h(k)=4h^{-2}\sum_j\sin^2(\pi k_j h)\ge16|k|^2$. Thus the time-integrated squared $L^2$ tail of the nodal trigonometric interpolant above $|k|>K$ is at most $C/K^2$, uniformly in $h,\tau$. Its distance in $L^2_{t,x}$ to the cell interpolant is $O(h)$. Indeed at any cell offset $\xi\in[0,h)^3$ discrete Parseval bounds the squared translation difference by $\sum_k |e^{2\pi i k\cdot\xi}-1|^2|\hat b(k)|^2\le C h^2\sum_k\lambda_h(k)|\hat b(k)|^2$. Average over offsets and sum in time. Complex interpolation at an even Nyquist frequency is harmless for this estimate. The cell interpolant is real.

**Time compactness of the resolved modes.** Write $M=\sup_h\|u_h^0\|_h$. For a fixed frequency and a unit polarization $q$, test one time step with the grid mode of polarization $P_h(k)q$, using real and imaginary parts. The solenoidal projection leaves the pairing with $b-a$ unchanged. The mode's forward difference and discrete Laplacian in the supremum norm are bounded by $C_k$ uniformly in $h$. Face averaging is an $L^2$ contraction in each direction. Hence $|c_h(a;b,v)|\le C_k M^2$. Summation by parts twice in the viscous term gives $|\langle D^+b,D^+v\rangle_h|=|\langle b,-\Delta_h v\rangle_h|\le C_k M$. It follows that $|\hat b(k)-\hat a(k)|\le \tau C_k(M^2+\nu_hM)$. The piecewise-linear time interpolants of each fixed mode are uniformly bounded and equi-Lipschitz. Arzelà–Ascoli and a diagonal subsequence yield uniform convergence of every fixed mode on bounded time intervals; endpoint versions differ there by $O_k(\tau)$. Together with the tail estimate this yields strong $L^2_{t,x}$ convergence of right-endpoint velocities. The energy identity bounds the time-integrated squared difference of the two endpoint interpolants by $\tau M^2$, giving the same strong limit on the left. Uniform energy and uniform low-mode convergence give a weakly continuous $L^2$ representative $u(t)$ and weak convergence of endpoints tending to each $t$, including $t=0$. For this last conclusion, pair first with a finite Fourier polynomial and use the uniform $L^2$ bound to control the test-function tail. Cell averages introduce vanishing offsets on each fixed test mode.

**Nonlinear momentum and initial trace.** The forward gradients of the right endpoints have a weak $L^2$ limit. Discrete summation by parts against smooth tests identifies it as $\nabla u$. Passing the centered divergence constraint against smooth scalar tests gives $\mathrm{div}\,u=0$. For each smooth time-dependent solenoidal test with finitely many spatial Fourier modes, sample the modes after applying $P_h(k)$ to their polarizations. The resulting tests lie exactly in $Z_h$, and their values, first differences, time differences and discrete Laplacians converge uniformly to their continuum counterparts. Time summation by parts gives the initial trace $u_0$ for tests vanishing at the final time. For any fixed direction the translated and face-averaged endpoint velocities converge strongly to $u$ in $L^2_{t,x}$: translation is an isometry on periodic cell fields and the limiting $u$ is continuous under spatial translations in $L^2$. The products $A_{ir} B_{ir}$ consequently converge to $u_j u$ in $L^1_{t,x}$. Against the forward difference of the test they give the term $-\int u_j u\cdot\partial_j v$. The viscous term converges by weak gradient convergence. The weak equation is therefore

$$-\int u\cdot\partial_t v-\int (u\otimes u):\nabla v+\nu\int\nabla u:\nabla v=\langle u_0,v(0)\rangle.$$

Fourier truncation extends the identity to all smooth periodic divergence-free tests. A periodic distributional pressure exists by projecting the residual onto spatial gradients (equivalently solving $-\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j)$ with zero mean). The formulation covers general three-dimensional data and includes the quadratic term.

**Energy inequality and global time.** After extraction, right endpoints converge strongly in spatial $L^2$ for almost every start time $s$, and initially by construction. Sum the finite energy identity from the grid index of that right endpoint to an endpoint tending to $t\ge s$. Apply weak lower semicontinuity to the terminal norm and the gradients on compact subintervals of $(s,t)$, then exhaust $(s,t)$. This gives $\|u(t)\|_2^2+2\nu\int_s^t\|\nabla u\|_2^2\le\|u(s)\|_2^2$ for every $t\ge s$ and almost every $s$, including the separately established case $s=0$. Weak continuity and the inequality yield strong continuity at zero. A diagonal subsequence on integer time horizons yields a global Leray–Hopf solution. This is a convergence result for this specific rational scheme. General weak existence is classical. A bound in a regularity class such as $L^4_t L^6_x$ for arbitrary smooth data remains open; the energy identity supplies $L^\infty_tL^2_x\cap L^2_tH^1_x$.

## 4. A positive-viscosity small-data capstone

For the scheme in §2 on the unit torus there is a mesh-independent, numerical sufficient condition. Set $X(v)=\|D^+v\|_{2,h}$, using the Euclidean sum over all directions and components. If $108X(u_h^0)\le\nu_h$, then for every step

$$X(b)^2+X(b-a)^2+\tau\nu_h\|\Delta_h b\|_{2,h}^2\le X(a)^2.$$

Consequently the limit is globally smooth for smooth divergence-free $u_0$ satisfying $108\|\nabla u_0\|_2<\nu$, with any constant mean. This threshold is sufficient; optimality is open for the estimate used here. Small-data regularity itself is classical. The construction provides a nonempty positive-viscosity capstone for the specified rational discretization and an explicit uniform constant.

**Uniform scalar Sobolev bound.** On the probability grid, each mean-zero scalar $f$ satisfies $\|f\|_{6,h}\le3\|D^+f\|_{2,h}$. Here is a proof with constants. On a line in direction $j$, the absolute value of $g$ at any point is at most the mean of $|g|$ on the line plus the sum of the absolute edge differences. Call this bound $G_j$ on the transverse indices. Thus $|g|^{3/2}\le(G_1G_2G_3)^{1/2}$. Normalized summation and Cauchy–Schwarz give $\mathbb E(G_1G_2G_3)^{1/2}\le(\mathbb E G_1\,\mathbb E G_2\,\mathbb E G_3)^{1/2}$: sum first over the common index of $G_2,G_3$, then apply Cauchy–Schwarz in the remaining two indices. The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality yields

$$\|g\|_{3/2,h}\le\|g\|_{1,h}+\tfrac13\sum_j\|D_j^+g\|_{1,h}.$$

Apply this to $g=|f|^4$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\|f\|_{4,h}^4\le\|f\|_{6,h}^3\|f\|_{2,h}$. The inequality $|a^4-b^4|\le2(|a|^3+|b|^3)|a-b|$ and translation invariance give $\|D^+_j|f|^4\|_{1,h}\le4\|f\|_{6,h}^3\|D_j^+f\|_{2,h}$. Cancel $\|f\|_{6,h}^3$ for nonzero $f$. The spectral estimate in §3 gives $\|f\|_{2,h}\le X(f)/4$ on mean-zero fields. Altogether the coefficient is $1/4+4/\sqrt3<3$.

**Nonlinear estimate.** Define $C_h(a,b)$ by $\langle C_h(a,b),v\rangle_h=c_h(a;b,v)$. For mean-zero $a\in Z_h$, zero face divergence cancels the cell-center term in its strong form. Each component is half the sum over $j$ of the outgoing face speed times $D_j^+b_\ell$ and the incoming speed times the backward difference. Each face speed has $L^6_h$ norm at most $3X(a)$. Each $D^+_j b_\ell$ has mean zero and $L^3_h$ norm at most $3\|\Delta_h b\|_{2,h}$. Indeed $L^3_h\le L^6_h$ on the probability grid and Parseval gives $\sum_{j,k}\|D_k^+D_j^+ b_\ell\|_{2,h}^2=\|\Delta_h b_\ell\|_{2,h}^2$. The backward difference is a translate. Hölder, the three directions, and the Euclidean sum over three components now give

$$\|C_h(a,b)\|_{2,h}\le27\sqrt3\,X(a)\|\Delta_h b\|_{2,h}\le54 X(a)\|\Delta_h b\|_{2,h}.$$

For general mean, write $a=\bar a+(a-\bar a)$. The constant part contributes $\sum_j\bar a_j D_j^0 b$, whose pairing with $-\Delta_h b$ vanishes since $D_j^0$ is skew-adjoint and commutes with the self-adjoint Laplacian. Thus the same **work** bound holds for arbitrary mean.

**Absorption and continuum regularity.** The test $-\Delta_h b$ belongs to $Z_h$ by commutation. Pair the step with it and use polarization on the forward gradients. Writing $Y=\|\Delta_h b\|_{2,h}^2$, transport work has magnitude at most $54X(a)Y$. When $108X(a)\le\nu_h$, at least half of the viscous dissipation remains after absorption. This proves the displayed inequality and propagates its assumption by induction; it requires no universal divisibility. Lean checks the finite three-plane sum and the fourth-power edge inequality used in the Sobolev argument. Its lemma `absorb_enstrophy` verifies the scalar absorption from the input work bound and the tested identity. The discrete Sobolev bound, grid work estimate and passage to regularity are written proofs.

For smooth data, cell averages have forward-gradient norm at most $\|\nabla u_0\|_2$ by Jensen's inequality along edges and averaging over cells. Choose rational approximations to those averages with pointwise error $o(h)$. Their gradient error is $o(1)$, and the rational projector $P_h$ commutes with differences and contracts $L^2_h$. Strict continuum smallness therefore gives the discrete inequality for every sufficiently fine grid. The scalar Sobolev estimate and conservation of the mean give a uniform $L^\infty_tL^6_x$ bound on cell interpolants. It passes to the limit in §3. This is inside the Serrin regularity range on each finite time interval. Together with smooth initial data and local strong existence, it gives a unique globally smooth solution. Uniqueness and subsequential compactness imply that the whole family converges in $L^2_{t,x}$ on every finite interval in this small-data regime.

**Eventual regularity for general finite-energy data.** The energy identity also bounds $2\nu_h\sum\tau X(u^{n+1})^2$ by the initial squared $L^2_h$ norm. If every right endpoint through a grid time $t$ violated $108X\le\nu_h$, this sum would exceed $2\nu_h t(\nu_h/108)^2$. Once one endpoint meets the threshold, the induction applies at every later step. For each fixed $t>108^2\|u_0\|_2^2/(2\nu^3)$, apply this argument first at an intermediate time strictly between this bound and $t$, then refine. The resulting Leray–Hopf limit is smooth after the displayed bound by the same local Serrin criterion. Possible loss of smoothness earlier for general smooth data remains the unsolved part. The waiting bound is sufficient and uses the full initial energy. Forcing is zero throughout.

Run `python3 scripts/verify_exact_transport.py` at the repository root and `lake build` under `lean/` with its pinned toolchain.
