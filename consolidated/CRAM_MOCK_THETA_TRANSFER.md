# CRAM and mock theta coefficients in the periodic Navier–Stokes transfer

CRAM can represent the rational finite-grid evolution exactly when each signed numerator has a certified range. The third-order mock theta function $\omega(q)$ supplies positive rational weights for a different viscosity stencil with the same nonlinear continuum limit. The role of the mock theta function in this construction is to select the jump coefficients. The proof of transfer uses their second moment, a uniform energy estimate and exact periodic identities.

## From a named mock theta function to a stencil

Use Watson's third-order notation

$$\omega(q)=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{q^{2m(m+1)}}{(q;q^2)_{m+1}^2}=\sum_{j\ge0}a_jq^j,\qquad (q;q^2)_{m+1}=\prod_{r=0}^{m}(1-q^{2r+1}).$$

Every coefficient is nonnegative by expanding the reciprocal squared factors. The first values are $a_0=1$, $a_1=2$, $a_2=3$, $a_3=4$ and $a_4=6$. For comparison, the third-order $f(q)=\sum_m q^{m^2}/(-q;q)_m^2$ has $[q^2]f=-2$, from the term $q/(1+q)^2$. It fails the positive jump-rate test under the same direct coefficient rule. This rules out a common Markov interpretation for both functions. [Chen and Wang, §4](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/529/11748-2020AIM-Representations_of_mock_theta_functions.pdf) · [Andrews and Yee](https://georgeandrews1.github.io/pdf/327.pdf)

Fix rational $0<q_*<1$, a cutoff $J\ge1$ and the weights

$$c_\ell=\frac{a_\ell q_*^\ell}{\sum_{r=1}^J r^2 a_rq_*^r},\qquad 1\le\ell\le J.$$

They satisfy $c_\ell\ge0$, $c_1>0$ and $\sum_\ell \ell^2c_\ell=1$. For $q_*=1/2$ and $J=2$, the unnormalized weights are $1$ and $3/4$. The resulting rational stencil on a period-$N$ grid with $h=1/N$ is

$$\Delta_{h,\omega} v(x)=h^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^3\left[\frac{v(x+e_j)-2v(x)+v(x-e_j)}4+\frac{3\big(v(x+2e_j)-2v(x)+v(x-2e_j)\big)}{16}\right].$$

Each fixed smooth mode has the correct second moment. For a smooth scalar field sampled on the grid, Taylor's theorem gives

$$\|\Delta_{h,\omega}v-\Delta v\|_{\infty,h}\le \frac{13}{48}h^2\sum_j\|\partial_j^4v\|_\infty.$$

The bound follows from $(c_1+16c_2)/12=13/48$. Across the whole grid spectrum, the positive symbol of $-\Delta_{h,\omega}$ is

$$\lambda_\omega(k)=4h^{-2}\sum_j\left(\tfrac14\sin^2(\pi h k_j)+\tfrac3{16}\sin^2(2\pi h k_j)\right).$$

Consequently $\lambda_{\rm nn}/4\le\lambda_\omega\le\lambda_{\rm nn}$ by $|\sin(2x)|\le2|\sin x|$. The stencil controls the high frequencies that the centered curl can miss. It commutes with all translations, hence with discrete centered curl and divergence. The nearest-neighbor term prevents period-two degeneracy.

The full $\omega$ series can enter through increasing cutoffs. Choose fixed $q_*<r<1$. The product $\prod_{s\ge0}(1-r^{2s+1})$ is positive since $\sum_s r^{2s+1}$ converges. The series $\omega(r)$ is finite because its terms are bounded by the reciprocal squared product times $r^{2m(m+1)}$. Thus $a_\ell q_*^\ell\le\omega(r)(q_*/r)^\ell$. For rational $r$, this majorant has an elementary rational upper bound. Take $K$ with $r^{2K+1}/(1-r^2)<1$ and use $\prod_{s\ge K}(1-r^{2s+1})\ge 1-r^{2K+1}/(1-r^2)$ for the tail product. Also $\sum_m r^{2m(m+1)}\le 1/(1-r^4)$. All moment tails are geometrically summable up to polynomial weights, the second-moment denominator is bounded above and bounded below by its $\ell=1$ term, and both the lower coercivity coefficient $c_1$ and the upper fourth moment bound are uniform in $J$. These estimates justify using $J=J(h)\to\infty$ at fixed $q_*$ with the same second-order local consistency bound. Taking $q_*\to1$ would require additional tail and coercivity analysis.

**A stencil from the original letter.** Ramanujan's January 12, 1920 letter also gives the third-order series $\psi(q)=\sum_{m\ge1}q^{m^2}/(q;q^2)_m$. Its first positive-degree coefficients are $1,1,1,2$. The same rule at $q_*=1/2$ and $J=2$ gives jump weights $c_1=1/3$ and $c_2=1/6$. Thus $\lambda_{\rm nn}/3\le\lambda_\psi\le\lambda_{\rm nn}$, the coefficient in the $O(h^2)$ Taylor defect is $1/4$, and the explicit CFL is $\nu\tau/h^2\le1/3$. At $\nu\tau/h^2=p/b$, the exact scale multiplier is $6b$ and the center, nearest and distance-two weights are $6b-18p$, $2p$ and $p$, respectively. The implicit proof below uses only positivity, normalization and a positive nearest coefficient, so it applies to this $\psi$ stencil as well. The reference verifies the full nonlinear $\psi$ step on a three-point-per-axis grid. [Ramanujan's letter, third-order examples](https://www.imsc.res.in/~rao/ramanujan/newnow/lastletter.pdf)

## Exact arithmetic and the nonlinear limit

For the two-jump stencil, let $\nu\tau/h^2=p/b$ with nonnegative integer $p$ and positive integer $b$. In the explicit diffusion update, multiply the common physical denominator by $16b$ and update numerators with center coefficient $16b-42p$, six nearest-neighbor coefficients $4p$, and six distance-two coefficients $3p$. Under $42p\le16b$ these coefficients are nonnegative and sum to $16b$. The normalized step preserves integer-field range intervals and the spatial mean. Applying it to the integer velocity witness and its raw centered curl gives the same new vorticity through either route, including when jumps wrap on the grid.

Each numerator operation is a linear combination of same-scale values and can be taken independently in every CRAM residue lane. Keep the common scale as part of the representation. The factors of two in $16b$ are nonunits in the primary CRT product $M=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13=30030$, so the scheme carries the denominator instead of inverting it in every lane. For each signed numerator, an independently verified interval of width smaller than the total effective modulus identifies one integer representative. For a symmetric bound $|V|\le B$, a sufficient condition is $2B<M$ or its certified extension with compatible sister-tray moduli. Carry no stored winding coordinate. An adjacent readout can derive a quotient from same-source observations and a certified window when that quotient is needed. An unchecked signed representative can wrap even though the modular stencil identity remains exact. These range certificates cover the chosen finite grid. Spatial refinement still needs initial data on the finer grid or a proved approximation of it. Exact residue readout cannot recover a fine-grid mode that was absent from the coarse observation.

For the full rational implicit velocity scheme, keep the existing conservative transport and use the bilinear viscosity form

$$\mathcal D_{h,\omega}(b,v)=\sum_{j=1}^3\sum_{\ell=1}^J c_\ell\left\langle \frac{b(\cdot+\ell e_j)-b}{h},\frac{v(\cdot+\ell e_j)-v}{h}\right\rangle_h.$$

The diagonal of the fixed-advector linear step is $\|z\|_{2,h}^2+\tau\nu_h\mathcal D_{h,\omega}(z,z)$ and transport still vanishes on the diagonal. Invertibility over the rationals and exact energy dissipation follow as before. A residue implementation of this solve needs more than the stencil lanes. After clearing denominators, an integer system may be singular modulo a prime even though it is invertible over the rationals. A claimed rational output can be verified by clearing its denominators, checking the resulting integer residual in the lanes, and certifying a range narrow enough that zero residue forces a zero integer residual. For $J=2$ the energy controls the nearest-neighbor gradient uniformly by $c_1=1/4$. The compactness proof using Fourier tails and fixed-mode time equicontinuity therefore applies. In the fixed-mode time estimate use the bounded symbol $\lambda_\omega(k)\le\lambda_{\rm nn}(k)$. Strong space-time convergence identifies the quadratic flux. For every fixed $\ell$, the difference quotient $[b(\cdot+\ell e_j)-b]/h$ converges weakly to $\ell\partial_j b$ by testing against smooth fields and periodic summation by parts. The viscous weak equation has coefficient $\nu$ by $\sum\ell^2c_\ell=1$. The same weak lower semicontinuity for the weighted jump gradients gives the full continuum viscous dissipation $\nu\int\|\nabla b\|_2^2$. The resulting subsequence is Leray–Hopf for arbitrary divergence-free finite-energy initial data. The perturbative argument with smooth samples also applies since the forward bound, skew transport and second-order viscosity defect still hold.

For $J(h)\to\infty$ at fixed $q_*$, the limit weights are $c_\ell^\infty$. For the weak viscous pairing one can move the generator to the smooth test and use the uniform second-order consistency just proved. For the energy inequality, first take the lower bound from a prescribed finite set of jumps, pass to the limit using weak convergence and $c_\ell\to c_\ell^\infty$, then exhaust the jump set by positivity. The full moment normalization persists by the common summable tail bound. This extends the written transfer to an increasing portion of the generating series. ## Relation to classical theta and modular completion

The continuous heat kernel on the one-dimensional unit torus is the classical Jacobi theta series $\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{-4\pi^2\nu t k^2}e^{2\pi i kx}$. Its product across coordinates is the heat kernel in three dimensions. Each fixed discrete symbol above converges to the corresponding continuum heat symbol, so the classical theta multiplier is recovered in the limit. Zwegers' nonholomorphic completion of mock theta functions uses period integrals of unary theta functions and a modular variable. The present construction uses the coefficients of the holomorphic series at a fixed rational damping parameter to build a spatial operator. An identification of the modular correction with a physical-time propagator would require an additional intertwining identity. The known completion theorem alone supplies no such identity. [Chinta, Jorgenson and Karlsson on heat kernels on tori](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.2014) · [Zwegers on mock theta completion](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4834)

The production N32 solver needs its own arithmetic and operator correspondence before inheriting this limit. For arbitrary large smooth data, global regularity still requires a continuation estimate or a validated solution enclosure.
